## Conversation decision trail

The identifiers below locate decisions by their distinctive wording and sequence, not by invented message timestamps.

### C01 | Starting organism and face

The creator described a fungal being made of mycelium, with a soft featureless light mould-green surface, no genitals, glassy endless white slightly bioluminescent eyes, and a mouth formed by a small gap in fungal tissue. **Effect:** material continuity, white unmarked eyes and a seam mouth are foundational. A generated humanoid surface detail is not automatically adopted.

### C02 | Internal filaments

The creator liked the proposed hyphae detail and requested successive prompt revisions. **Effect:** fine internal branching became part of the material description. Later visibility and structure corrections take priority over the brightness of early renders.

### C03 | Points replace feet

The creator tried “legs ... more spindly, ending in points instead of feet” and asked for growth that grips the ground during each step. **Effect:** original feet and lower-leg bands are historical, not present-day leg requirements. Movement becomes a separate contact process.

### C04 | Hair becomes fine lichen without changing its outline

After the long-lichen image, the creator asked to “keep the same hair shape,” make the lichen smaller and add darker accents. Later wording fixed dark edges/extremities and washed-out interiors. **Effect:** compact bob and small lichen texture both matter. R06's long growth is rejected as a hair direction.

### C05 | Feature-specific selection

The creator said R08 got the leg proportions and hair texture/feel right, while preferring the earlier branching at contact points. **Effect:** this explicitly selects a combination, not a single whole image. R08 does not settle the contact web, cap height or glowing-node detail.

### C06 | Preserve the intact point through contact

After continued dissatisfaction with sprouting, the working description separated the point from hyphae emerging laterally just above contact. The creator requested tests of this description. **Effect:** the current mechanism specifies a clean intact point and temporary external growth; not a point replaced by a root bundle.

### C07 | Remove the remaining foot silhouette

The creator observed that some upper examples still looked like feet. The next description required a continuous lower-leg taper with no heel, sole or forward bend. **Effect:** “pointed” cannot mean an ordinary foot posed on tiptoe.

### C08 | Reduce calves while retaining chosen proportions

After R11 the creator called the result nearly perfect, then asked to thin the calf and combine it with the earlier proportional reference. **Effect:** R11's terminal geometry is useful but its calf volume is not the final target. Pair it with R08 and the explicit thinning instruction.

### C09 | Less constellation-like hyphae

The creator asked: “Maybe make the hyphae look less like constellations.” **Effect:** remove bright junction dots, star-like nodes, straight geometric networking and an overall decorative light-net reading. R13 is an attempted response and retains imperfections.

### C10 | Restrained expressions

The creator said some expressions felt off, requested another attempt using the proposed restrained rules, then asked for an expression-only sheet. **Effect:** keep eye and mouth construction stable and shift expression toward head, posture and hands. Later expression panels are working references, not all individually approved.

### C11 | Separate the documents

The creator requested a more condensed master prompt, with locomotion kept separate, then a locomotion page and reference images. **Effect:** maintain a concise core prompt and a separate behaviour specification. Do not rebuild one enormous all-purpose prompt.

### C12 | Correct the cap and make its description standalone

The creator warned that the cap in the turnaround was drifting, specified fly agaric as its basis, and asked to refine the description for use in isolation. The resulting broad, shallow mature fly-agaric description was requested for the master prompt and design brief. **Effect:** the latest written low-profile cap supersedes the taller generated crown. R19 is not a canonical turnaround.

### C13 | Separate visual identity from art style

The current request requires visual attributes translatable to any style and a comprehensive resource for creation and maintenance. **Effect:** remove mandatory anime/painterly/realistic rendering, forest light, scenery and decorative layout from the physical design brief; retain physical material and colour relationships. Put presentation choices and workflow guidance elsewhere.

## Conflicts handled explicitly

| Conflict | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Original feet versus later points | Later explicit pointed-leg decision wins. Original remains archived as origin evidence. |
| Selected leg length versus bulkier successful contact studies | Combine the selected proportion, later calf thinning and uninterrupted-point geometry by feature. |
| High cap peaks in most images versus later broad/shallow wording | Latest cap text wins; the visual turnaround remains unvalidated. |
| Beautiful bright networks versus “less like constellations” | Preserve fine organic branching, not bright nodes or geometric regularity. |
| Captions saying “release” while strands remain attached | The final airborne state must satisfy the written clean-point rule. |
| Ordinary hair plus lichen decoration versus hair made of lichen-like tissue | Use small lichen growth to form the compact bob itself. |
| Generated anatomy or expressive mouths versus the original minimal organism | Do not import unrequested human-specific detail or broad smiles. |
| Style words mixed with colour/material descriptions | Keep base colour and material response; move medium, light and setting to production. |

## Maintenance procedure

Start with the current design brief. Record a proposed change and the exact feature it affects. Compare a new asset with the relevant feature references and run the review checklist. Seek creator approval for substantive design changes. Record the approval's scope, update the brief or appropriate behaviour guide, then synchronise the master prompt and reference index.

Keep old assets as development history. A newer image is not automatically a more authoritative image. Do not change several uncertain features merely because one generated result looks cohesive.

**Request sequence:** The creator proposed replacing Myca's synthetic-looking skirt with a thin pink fungal membrane that reads as an extension of her body, then refined the construction to remove the belt/waistband and make the membrane visibly sprout from and blend into her tissue. Subsequent requests established three initial candidate architectures and four additional candidates.

**Candidate set:**

- Single-sheet membrane.
- Vascular-reinforced membrane.
- Gill-frill membrane.
- Veil-streamer membrane.
- Spore-lace membrane.

**Shared construction rule for membrane tasks:** No belt, waistband, fastening, ordinary fabric seam or draped-on garment boundary. Pale-green body tissue transitions organically into dusty-pink living membrane and subtle hyphae continue into the structure. The candidates differ in growth architecture, not merely texture or trim.

**Approval scope:** These seven designs are **additional candidates / active experiments**, not a creator selection of a replacement. The conventional dusty-pink flared skirt with dark brown waistband remains the default for unrelated work. No membrane candidate is canonical unless the creator explicitly promotes it.

**Protected features:** This experiment does not revise Myca's cap, face, lichen bob, body material, proportions, pointed leg geometry or locomotion. Generated membrane sheets may contain drift in those areas and must not be used as blanket references.
