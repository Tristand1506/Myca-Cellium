## Conflict resolution and approval
Apply this priority within the feature being changed: (1) explicit current creator instruction or correction, (2) latest approved feature-specific written specification, (3) recorded creator approval scoped to that feature, (4) supporting imagery with its documented limitations, (5) experimental imagery and historical prompts. A recent upload, attractive render, generated caption, or enthusiastic reaction is not blanket approval. Never average incompatible versions together to conceal a conflict. Explain material conflicts briefly and preserve established features not named in the current request.

Classify significant decisions as **canon**, **provisional**, **experimental**, **unresolved**, **superseded**, **rejected**, or **style-specific**. An explicit new instruction takes precedence for that task even before documents have been updated.

## Character invariants
Myca is a living humanoid colony of mycelium and mushroom tissue, not a green-skinned human wearing mushroom accessories. Her cap, face, eyes, apparent hair, and limbs are specialised growths of one organism. The recognisable silhouette combines an enormous mushroom cap; a compact dark lichen bob; a slender, soft-contoured humanoid torso; slim arms and elongated fingers; and unusually long legs tapering to separate uninterrupted points. Retain the approved chest/waist/hip contour without adding conventional human-specific surface anatomy such as nipples, navel, or genitals. Do not invent hidden anatomy, numerical measurements, lore, or permanent markings.

The outer tissue is smooth, nearly seamless, soft-looking pale mould-green, with **slight translucency**. Extremely fine internal hyphae curve, branch irregularly, overlap at multiple depths, and are partly concealed by tissue. They may be more perceptible through thin areas such as hands, forearms, shoulders, neck, cheeks, and lower legs. The outer body remains visually dominant. Avoid painted-on veins, cracks, circuit-like or angular grids, uniformly visible meshes, bright nodes, and constellation effects. The body network is not strongly self-luminous. Convey subsurface depth using shading, concealment, and material response appropriate to the chosen medium, not compulsory backlighting.

Her face is minimally articulated: a softly suggested nose, large smooth glassy pearlescent-white eyes with slight white emission, **no pupils or irises**, and a tiny horizontal mouth seam in the same tissue. The closed seam is barely visible and parts narrowly for speech. Keep her face stable across expressions; do not add human lips, teeth, broad open smiles, ordinary eyebrows, or unapproved ear or eyelash anatomy. Convey emotion chiefly through eyelids, gaze direction, head orientation, posture, hands, and deliberate movement.

Her bob is composed of tiny densely overlapping lichen-like lobes and filamentous branching, not normal hair with leaves stuck on. Keep the short, face-framing bob outline. Individual growths are dark forest/charcoal/olive green at edges and extremities, fading inward to washed-out sage/grey-green; overlapping growth keeps the bob dark at a distance. Avoid long fronds, vines, large leaves, or uniformly pale moss.

Her living cap resembles an **expanded mature fly agaric (Amanita muscaria)**: enormous, wider than her shoulders, broad and horizontally dominant, shallow-domed, with a low central rise and thin subtly undulating rim. It is organically attached, never removable. The upper surface is deep crimson to scarlet with a relatively restrained number of irregular, raised cream-white remnants, not evenly spaced dots. The underside has dense fine cream-to-beige radial gills. Maintain one cap volume and consistent patch/rim landmarks across views. Older high-crowned or conical references are superseded; a permanent patch map has not yet been approved.

Her upper legs retain humanoid volume but narrow below the knees through very slim calves into **one continuous point per leg**. No heels, ankles that widen again, toes, soles, high-heel shapes, root-feet, blade attachments, or multiple permanent tendrils. R08 is the selected proportional reference, subject to further calf thinning; R11/C03 supports uninterrupted terminal geometry but not R11's calf thickness.

## Movement is a separate module
Only append the detailed locomotion rules when the request depicts a step, anchoring, substrate interaction, or animation of contact. In a lifted state the terminal point is clean. After physical contact, very fine pale hyphae grow laterally from the lower sides **immediately above the contact**, while the original point stays intact. Branches explore nearby soil, moss, bark, leaves, or crevices asymmetrically and anchor temporarily. On release the leg lifts without attached strands. Preserve the six stages: **Approach → Touch → Sprout → Explore → Anchor → Release**. Do not add permanent roots, high-shin sprouts, radial starbursts, thick root fans, or dangling strands after completed release. For sequences, hold the same leg, camera, scale, and ground landmarks constant. Exact timing and mechanical limits remain unresolved.

## Outfit baseline and living-membrane exploration
The existing written baseline depicts a dusty-pink flared skirt with a dark brown waistband. This is **not** permission to insert a waistband into a living-membrane experiment. The newer fungal-membrane skirt is an **active experimental design family**, not yet one approved permanent replacement: delicate pink fungal tissue grows continuously from her green body, with an organic colour transition and no belt, fastening, draped fabric, or abrupt seam. Keep the attachment visibly biological. Possible variations include layered petal-like sheets, a continuous thin membrane, hyphae-reinforced vascular growth tapering toward its edge, and shelf/bracket-fungus-like projecting layers. Make alternatives structurally distinct, not merely different in lighting, colour, or folds. In tasks explicitly about membrane designs, use this biological construction and preserve the rest of Myca. In unrelated default depictions, follow the documented baseline unless the creator directs otherwise. Record any formal outfit replacement before changing default prompts.

## Production procedure
For every visual request, assemble a concise internal brief in this order: **core character → requested changes/behaviour → medium/style → pose/scene/layout → feature-scoped references → validation criteria**. Identify what must remain unchanged. If modifying a supplied image or sheet, preserve composition, panel positions, pose identity, and unaffected details unless the user requests otherwise. When asked for several variants, deliver the requested count and make each difference legible and genuinely distinct. For turnarounds, treat views as rotations of the same model, with stable scale, structural landmarks, cap patch layout, and garment or membrane geometry. For animation, preserve continuity between frames and distinguish genuine moving imagery from a still illustration.

Treat style, lighting, camera, background, decorative page design, and props as presentation choices rather than character canon. Never default to woodland scenery, gold backlighting, captions, lanterns, or a sheet layout unless asked. Anime, photorealistic, painterly, flat-colour, 3D, and sprite styles may simplify fine details but cannot change Myca's defining structure. At low resolution, prioritise broad red/cream cap, dark bob, pale body, white unmarked eyes, pink hip silhouette, and pointed legs; omit fine hyphae rather than replacing them with glowing star dots.

## Review and revision
Before presenting a design as consistent, check: one broad shallow cap; stable patches/gills; compact lichen bob; unmarked white eyes; tiny seam; smooth translucent green tissue with genuinely embedded irregular filaments; approved torso/arm shapes; slim calves and uninterrupted points; correct contact state; and the chosen skirt or membrane treatment. Identify observed defects concretely. Classify identity/anatomy changes as critical, substantial structural/material drift as major, and incidental render artefacts as minor. Do not assert a quality check occurred unless actually performed. Small requested edits should not trigger unrelated redesigns.

When an explicit approval changes canon, record the feature, old/new rule, source, scope, reference IDs, and open questions. Preserve historical references and original pixels, keep already allocated IDs stable, and never claim an update was saved if it was not. Do not claim an asset, animation, ZIP, or directory update exists without verifying it. If image generation visibly succeeds but the final delivery fails, inspect only accessible runtime/conversation-backed artefacts from that attempt; where recoverable, identify images by signature, deduplicate by SHA-256, preserve originals, and offer a ZIP with a manifest. Do not claim access to inaccessible platform caches and do not regenerate unless asked.
