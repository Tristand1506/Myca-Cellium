# Myca | Character Design Brief

**Design baseline 1.0 · 8 October 2026 · Style-independent visual specification**

This document defines what Myca looks like and what her visible structures are. It does not prescribe a medium, drawing style, rendering technique, camera, lighting setup or background. Her appearance must remain recognisable when those presentation choices change. Locomotion, acting and production instructions have their own pages.

The baseline consolidates the creator's decisions and the supplied descriptions. It does not declare every detail in a generated image correct. Read the [Reference Index](References/Reference%20Index.md) before using an image; each reference has a limited purpose and specific caveats. Unresolved details are identified rather than silently invented.

## 1. Identity and construction

Myca is a living fungal colony composed of mycelium and mushroom tissue, grown into a humanoid form. Her body, face, eyes, cap and lichen-like growth belong to the same organism. The cap is not clothing, and the hair is not ordinary hair with fungal accessories attached.

Her structure comprises a head beneath a very large cap, a humanoid torso, two slender arms with elongated fingers, and two long legs narrowing to individual points. The original soft chest, waist and hip contours remain part of the silhouette; fungal construction does not mean replacing those contours with a generic stick figure or a plant stem. Conversely, a humanoid contour is not permission to add conventional human anatomy.

The creator's starting description specifies a soft, featureless pale mould-green surface and no genitals. Human-specific surface features that appear incidentally in generated images, including nipples or a navel, are not established design additions. Preserve the approved overall contours without treating those incidental marks as requirements. Detailed hidden anatomy has not been defined.

**Evidence:** creator's original description and drawing R01; prior brief S01, Character Concept / Body Material; prior prompt S02, Core Biology. The statement above retains the creator's original anatomical constraint rather than inferring it from later renders.

## 2. Overall silhouette and proportion relationships

Myca's largest horizontal element is her mushroom cap. It extends substantially beyond her head and is wider than her shoulders. A compact bob sits beneath it and frames the face. Her torso and arms are comparatively narrow. A flared skirt creates a second, smaller width around the hips. Below the skirt, the legs are long and progressively narrow, ending in two separate continuous points.

The upper legs keep the volume needed for the established humanoid outline. Below the knees, the lower legs become markedly slender, with little calf bulge. The terminal taper is long, not a short spike added to an otherwise ordinary foot.

The creator selected R08 for leg proportions and hair texture, then asked for further calf thinning after the continuous-point study R11. These are complementary approvals: use R08 for the overall proportion relationship, the calf-thinning instruction for reduced lower-leg bulk, and R11 for the uninterrupted terminal geometry. Do not copy R11's calf volume wholesale.

Absolute height, body-to-head ratios, cap diameter and exact limb measurements have not been fixed. No measurements sampled from perspective images are canonical. A production model may use documented working measurements, but they need approval before becoming part of this baseline.

## 3. Fungal surface and internal mycelium

Myca's outer tissue is pale mould-green, smooth, soft-looking and nearly seamless. It has slight translucency, not glass-like transparency. Its interior is only partly visible. The outer form remains easy to read rather than dissolving into a dense line pattern.

Internal hyphae are extremely fine filaments distributed through the tissue. Their paths curve, branch unevenly, overlap and pass at different depths. Some are hidden by the outer material; others become perceptible through thinner areas around the hands, forearms, shoulders, neck, face and lower legs. This variation is a material property, not a requirement for a particular lighting setup.

The network is not a printed decoration or a pattern of cracks. Branch intersections do not form bright dots, stars or regularly spaced junctions. Filaments are not an angular wireframe, circuit diagram or evenly distributed net. Their visibility is subordinate to the body surface.

The latest direction favours restrained filament visibility. Strong self-lighting is not a defining property of the body's network. The eyes, by contrast, have explicitly established low-intensity white bioluminescence. Earlier bright network renders should not override that distinction.

**Evidence:** S01, Body Material; S02, Core Biology; creator's request to make the hyphae less like constellations. R13 is an attempted correction, not proof that all node-like artefacts disappeared.

## 4. Head and face

Myca's face has continuous pale-green fungal tissue, a softly suggested nose, minimal facial contouring and a small mouth seam. It is not defined by realistic human skin texture, strong nasal openings or separately coloured lips.

The mouth is a tiny, predominantly horizontal separation in the same tissue as the face. Closed, it is barely visible. During speech, it parts into a narrow opening and then settles together again. It does not acquire protruding lip volumes, a broad lip outline or a large open smiling shape. Visible teeth are not part of the established expression design. Fine nearby hyphae may converge toward the seam without a glowing radial burst.

The eyes are larger visual features than the mouth. The relative position of the eyes, the small mouth and the bob must remain consistent across angles and expressions. A new expression must not become a new face.

The working expression rules omit conventional eyebrows. Some images introduce brows, eyelashes, stronger noses or distinctly human ears. Those renderings do not independently establish additional design requirements. Exact ear shape, eyelash treatment and detailed eye-rim construction remain unresolved; do not silently promote a generated example into canon.

## 5. Eyes

Each eye is a smooth, glassy, uninterrupted pearlescent-white form. There are no pupils, irises or contrasting internal markings. A reflection is a surface reflection, not a pupil. The white interior has apparent depth rather than reading as a flat painted disc.

The eyes emit a small amount of white light. They do not need to illuminate the entire face or change colour. Their volume remains present beneath eyelids; modest changes in eyelid openness can support expression without redrawing the eyes into symbolic shapes.

The same eye construction applies in neutral poses, surprise, affection and speech. Detailed blink timing and any emotion-driven change in brightness have not been locked.

## 6. Lichen bob

Myca's apparent hair is lichen-like fungal growth arranged within a compact, short bob silhouette. It falls beside the face and remains close to the head and jaw. The outer mass reads as predominantly dark; close examination reveals small overlapping, finely branching structures rather than ordinary strands decorated with a few leaves.

The growth combines thin leaf-like lobes and filamentous branching forms. Individual structures are small and dense enough to preserve the short haircut outline. They must not become a long hanging curtain, vine hairstyle, large leafy mane or separate botanical ornaments.

The colour transition is defined **within each growth**, not merely from the roots to the tips of an ordinary hairstyle: outer edges and extremities are dark forest-green, charcoal-green, muted olive or nearly black, becoming progressively paler and less saturated toward the interior. Central areas and fine inner branching appear washed-out sage or grey-green. Overlap supplies the darker overall mass.

Keep both scales readable: a compact dark bob at character scale, and fine dark-edged, pale-centred lichen at detail scale. Do not average the entire bob into pale moss, and do not substitute black human hair carrying unrelated pale fronds.

**Evidence:** explicit small-lichen / same-hair-shape correction; creator's selection of R08's texture and feel; S01, Lichen Hair. No real lichen species or biological symbiosis has been specified.

## 7. Mushroom cap

Myca's cap is a living part of her body, based on the **expanded mature form of a fly agaric, Amanita muscaria**. This is the established visual reference for its design, not an assertion that every feature of Myca duplicates a real mushroom.

### Shape and connection

The cap is enormous relative to the head, wider than the shoulders, and predominantly horizontal. A low central rise flows into a broad shallow dome. It has an expansive overhang rather than a narrow or tall crown. Its rim becomes thin at the edge and has gentle, irregular undulation, with small local rises and droops. These variations are part of one continuous cap.

From the front, it reads as a wide canopy with a modest central rise. From the side, it retains depth and a shallow dome; it must not collapse into a narrow triangular hat. From above, the footprint is broadly circular but subtly irregular rather than a perfect geometric disc. The cap connects organically into the top of the head; it has no hatband, separate wearable brim or removal mechanism.

This latest written description takes priority over taller central peaks in the original drawing and generated sheets. Do not average the high-crowned and low-profile versions together. The creator requested the standalone fly-agaric description and then asked for it to be incorporated into the master prompt.

### Upper surface

The upper surface is deep crimson to scarlet with modest natural variation. It carries a relatively small number of prominent, irregular cream-white patches plus occasional smaller fragments. Patches vary in shape, spacing, thickness and size. They are softly raised fungal remnants, not evenly arranged painted polka dots.

The exact number and placement of patches have not been fixed. For any individual model or turnaround, retain a chosen patch layout consistently as the cap rotates. A landmark map may be proposed and approved later.

### Underside and edge

The underside contains densely packed fine gills arranged radially from the central attachment toward the edge. Their base colour is cream to pale beige. Thin edge tissue allows some light transmission; the edge need not be permanently luminous. The gills are tissue structures, not hanging hair or decorative ribbing.

**Evidence:** latest creator-approved standalone cap revision; S01 and S02, Mushroom Cap. R19 is specifically a drift example, not an approved turnaround. No existing complete turnaround has been confirmed against this updated cap specification.

## 8. Arms and hands

Myca's arms are slender. Her fingers are elongated and end in softly tapered fingertips. The hands share the same smooth pale-green material as the rest of the body; their thinner tissue makes the internal filament network easier to perceive.

Hand details must not add ordinary skin texture, vascular markings or a separate material at the wrist. Exact nail treatment and fine hand topology have not been explicitly standardised. Preserve consistency within a production model rather than inheriting a different hand from each generated panel.

## 9. Legs and terminal points

Her upper legs retain the established humanoid volume. Below the knee, the leg becomes progressively narrower with very little calf bulk. From approximately mid-calf downward, the entire lower limb forms an elongated continuous taper ending in **one point**.

Front, rear and side contours converge toward the same terminal region. The end does not bend forward, widen, flatten underneath or form a rear projection. There is no foot, ankle-shaped narrowing followed by expansion, heel, arch, sole or toe. The single point is not a high-heeled foot or a foot standing on tiptoe.

The leg remains one continuous pale-green volume. It is not a bundle of roots, a frayed stalk, a blade attached to a calf or several permanent tendrils. When airborne, the point has no external filaments. Temporary contact growth is a separate behaviour, not part of the permanent leg silhouette.

**Evidence:** R08 proportion selection; the request to thin the calf after R11; S03, Canonical Leg Shape. See the separate [Locomotion Reference](Myca%20Locomotion%20Reference.md) for contact behaviour.

## 10. Clothing and optional props

Myca's base outfit has a soft dusty-pink skirt and a dark brown waistband. The skirt begins at the waist and flares over the hips, with a free lower edge and folds that respond to pose. Standing references generally place its hem above the knees; the precise hemline, number of layers, seams and fastening have not been locked.

Its colour, waist position and overall flared mass are more established than any individual generated fold pattern. Do not make the skirt permanently transparent because some backlit versions appear translucent. Fine tailoring decisions require a production specification rather than guesswork from lighting.

A small lantern is a recurring optional prop from the original drawing. It is not attached to the body and is not required in every depiction. Small mushrooms, plants and woodland items are recurring subjects of her attention, not mandatory accessories. The small purple mushroom appearing in several images has no confirmed identity or companion status.

## 11. Intrinsic colour relationships

| Part | Established visual colour | Relationship to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Outer fungal tissue | Pale mould-green | Light body mass; not grey stone or saturated neon green |
| Eyes | Pearlescent white | Unmarked light forms; low white emission |
| Lichen edges / extremities | Forest-green, charcoal-green, dark olive, near-black | Darker and richer than each growth's interior |
| Lichen interiors | Washed-out sage and grey-green | Pale, desaturated internal areas within a dark bob |
| Cap upper surface | Deep crimson to scarlet | Red dominant mass, distinct from the pink skirt |
| Cap patches and gills | Cream-white patches; cream to pale-beige gills | Irregular light patches over red; light underside |
| Skirt and waistband | Dusty pink; dark brown | Pink lower garment with a darker waist boundary |

These are descriptive base colours. Warm golden light, forest-green reflections and photographed colour swatches are not additional intrinsic colours. No HEX, RGB, print or material values have been approved. Lighting and medium may alter the appearance, but should not redefine the base relationships.

## 12. What is not a design requirement

Anime conventions, painterly realism, photorealism, line weight, shading method, bloom, volumetric rays, depth of field, warm backlighting, a woodland background, a parchment board, botanical ornament, labels, slogans and layout are presentation choices. They are intentionally absent from the physical specification above.

The character's soft tissue, glassy eyes, material translucency and fine structural branching **are** visual properties. How an artist represents those properties is separate. The [Production Guide](Production/Prompt%20Modules%20and%20Style%20Translation.md) provides optional implementation guidance without making it canon.

## Standalone description

Myca is a humanoid fungal colony with smooth, slightly translucent pale mould-green tissue and a faint internal network of fine, curved, irregularly branching filaments. Her minimal face has glassy pearlescent-white eyes with no pupils or irises and a tiny horizontal mouth seam. A compact dark bob consists of miniature lichen-like growths, dark at their edges and washed-out sage-grey toward their centres. An enormous living cap grows from her head: a broad, shallow, mature fly-agaric canopy, wider than her shoulders, with a low central rise, gently irregular thin rim, crimson-red surface, uneven cream-white patches and closely spaced cream radial gills underneath. Her arms and fingers are slender. Her legs retain upper-leg volume but narrow through very slim calves into long continuous points, without feet or permanent tendrils. She wears a dusty-pink flared skirt and a dark brown waistband. Temporary ground-contact growth and expressive movement are specified separately.
