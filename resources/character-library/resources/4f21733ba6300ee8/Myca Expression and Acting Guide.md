# Myca | Expression and Acting Guide

**Baseline 1.0 · 8 October 2026 · Performance guidance, separate from physical design**

## Established intent

Myca is gentle, curious, patient, observant and quietly intelligent. She pays attention to small organisms and environmental details. These are character and performance attributes, not a requirement for one drawing style or one facial expression.

Her face is intentionally minimal. Emotion is carried by modest eyelid changes, minute mouth-seam movement, head direction, neck and shoulder position, body lean and hand placement. Her broad attached cap makes head-angle changes especially visible in the silhouette. That does not give the cap an independent emotional motion or a detachable joint.

**Sources:** S01, Face / Personality; S02, Face / Personality and Acting; the conversation's restrained-expression rules following the first animation-sheet critique. R16 is a working visual study, not an entirely approved expression set.

## Consistency rules

The face remains the same face. Keep the pearlescent-white unmarked eyes, small mouth seam and compact lichen bob across all states. Eye volume does not flatten into a symbol, pupils do not appear to indicate gaze, and the mouth does not acquire human lips, teeth or a broad grin.

A small eyelid change is different from remodelling the eye. The mouth may make a fractional curvature change or part narrowly, but should remain much less visually dominant than the eyes. Conventional eyebrows are excluded by the working expression rule.

Gaze direction is communicated through head orientation and attention to a subject, since there are no pupils to turn toward it. Hands can clarify curiosity, hesitation or care. The lichen may settle with motion; independent waving, mood-based growth or active antenna-like behaviour has not been established.

## Expression direction bank

These are reusable acting directions distilled from the discussion, not rigid poses, measured facial blendshapes or new anatomy.

| State | Primary acting cue | Facial limit |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral | Level head; unforced shoulders and hands. | Mouth almost invisible; eyes unchanged. |
| Curious | Small sideways head tilt and a lean toward the subject; one hand may approach. | Slight increase in eyelid openness, not larger eyeballs. |
| Gentle amusement | Subtle softening of posture; a small downward head inclination. | At most a minute upward seam change. |
| Delighted | Posture lifts and hands draw inward toward what caught her attention. | No large smile or symbolic closed eyes; brighter emission is not required. |
| Thoughtful | Head lowers toward the subject; a hand can pause near the chin. | Same eye and mouth construction. |
| Concerned | Mild shoulder tension and a careful, hesitant reach or withdrawn hand. | Slight eyelid and seam adjustments, not a deeply remodelled brow. |
| Startled | Brief stillness or recoil; lifted shoulders and a hand response. | Slightly wider eyelids; the mouth remains small. |
| Speaking | An appropriate conversational gesture and attention toward the listener. | Narrow seam opening without teeth or lip volume. |
| Fond / affectionate | Relaxed shoulders and a small head tilt toward the subject. | Quiet softening rather than a conventional broad smile. |
| Focused | Head directed at one task; deliberate precise hand placement. | Level seam and consistent white eyes. |
| Resting | Released posture with minimal gesture. | Eyelids may lower; a final blink/closed-eye system is not yet standardised. |

## Whole-body performance

Recurring actions include studying a small mushroom, carrying a lantern, crouching beside an organism, examining a detail between the fingers and pausing to observe the environment. These support the established temperament. They do not establish new powers, a profession, a backstory or ownership of recurring props.

Maintain the pointed-leg geometry in sitting and crouching poses as well as walking. A pose should not solve contact by quietly growing ordinary feet. Use the locomotion specification when contact mechanics need to be visible, not an improvised root-foot pose.

## Expression-sheet production

Use a small set of clearly separated expressions first, with enough space for the cap, face and relevant hand gesture. Keep consistent eye spacing, scale and material, and distinguish changes in camera angle from changes in expression. A fixed neutral face beside the variants makes identity drift easier to spot.

Label the actual expression shown. Do not accept a panel because its caption says “subtle” or “small seam.” The visible pixels must satisfy the rule. R16's speaking-mouth inset and some smiles remain examples to refine, not mandatory mouth shapes.

## Still unresolved

Exact facial dimensions, mouth-opening limits, a speech/phoneme system, blink timing, final eyelash treatment, eye-brightness animation and lichen secondary-motion settings have not been approved. They belong in a future animation implementation specification, with changes checked against the physical brief.

## Standalone expression module

Keep Myca's minimal fungal face structurally consistent across all expressions: glassy pearlescent-white eyes without pupils or irises, no conventional eyebrows, and a tiny horizontal mouth seam. Communicate emotion primarily through small eyelid changes, head angle, the attached cap's orientation, shoulder position, posture and hands. Speaking only parts the seam narrowly. Do not introduce broad smiles, visible teeth, human lips, symbolic eye shapes or a new face for each emotion. Her acting is curious, patient and attentive; use precise small gestures rather than relying on large facial deformation.
