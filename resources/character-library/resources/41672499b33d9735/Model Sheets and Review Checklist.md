**Production guidance · Review every asset against the same design baseline**

## Review at three scales

First inspect a small silhouette: cap breadth, compact bob, flared skirt and the long pointed legs should remain recognisable. Next inspect the full figure: calf volume, upper/lower proportion relationships, cap height and face identity. Finally inspect details: lichen scale, hyphal shape, seam construction and contact origins.

A beautiful rendering can fail any of these checks. Detail quality does not compensate for changed anatomy.

## Required visual checks

### Silhouette and body

- [ ] The cap is wider than the shoulders and remains a broad shallow canopy.
- [ ] Upper-leg volume is retained without a pronounced lower-calf bulge.
- [ ] Each lower leg continuously narrows into one point, including in side view.
- [ ] No heel, forward foot bend, sole, toe, frayed terminal bundle or permanent root-foot appears.
- [ ] The dusty-pink skirt and dark brown waistband retain the base outfit relationship.
- [ ] Additional human-specific surface anatomy is not being copied from generated artefacts.

### Cap and lichen

- [ ] Cap height has not crept toward a tall cone or triangular hat.
- [ ] Its rim, attachment and patch landmarks describe one consistent object across views.
- [ ] Cream patches are irregular, not an even field of identical dots.
- [ ] Gills occupy the underside radially and do not become hair.
- [ ] The bob stays short and compact; fine lichen makes up the hair mass.
- [ ] Dark-edged, pale-centred growth is preserved without enlarged fronds or a leafy mane.

### Surface and face

- [ ] Internal hyphae are fine, curved, uneven and partially hidden, without luminous intersection nodes.
- [ ] Eyes stay white and unmarked; a highlight has not turned into a pupil.
- [ ] The mouth remains a small seam, including when speaking or smiling.
- [ ] Eyelid changes do not produce a different eye construction.
- [ ] No new brows, ears, eyelashes, nails or oral anatomy are silently being treated as established.

### Contact behaviour, when depicted

- [ ] The airborne point is clean before and after the step.
- [ ] Growth starts only after contact, from lower sides immediately above the substrate.
- [ ] The tip remains intact rather than splitting or becoming the web.
- [ ] Branches are much finer than the leg, asymmetric and close to the terrain.
- [ ] A sequence keeps the same leg, view, scale and terrain landmarks.
- [ ] Weight transfer occurs during anchoring, and release is visually completed.

### Presentation and metadata

- [ ] Medium, lighting, setting and layout are recorded as production choices, not new canon.
- [ ] Captions match the actual image; generated labels have been checked manually.
- [ ] The reference IDs and the features borrowed from each are recorded.
- [ ] Known defects and unresolved decisions are written down.
- [ ] The asset is not marked “approved” without an explicit creator decision and approval scope.

## Acceptance rules

**Pass:** the asset satisfies the relevant settled rules for its intended use. Low-detail work may omit detail that cannot survive its scale, but cannot contradict the silhouette or mechanism.

**Needs revision:** the intended rule is present but visually ambiguous, or a known generator artefact remains. Record the affected feature rather than approving the entire asset.

**Exploration:** the asset tests a proposed variation. Keep it separate from selected references until the creator chooses a feature.

**Superseded / drift:** a later decision replaces the feature, or the creator has already challenged it. Preserve it as history, not as a production target.

These review labels describe the result of a check. They do not grant creator approval by themselves.
