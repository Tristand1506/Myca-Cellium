# Myca | Prompt Modules and Style Translation

**Production guidance · Not additional character canon**

## Separate the character from the picture

Assemble a task in this order: **character definition + requested behaviour + presentation + feature-scoped references + review criteria**. The physical design does not change because the final asset is line art, a miniature, a low-resolution sprite, an animation model or a rendered illustration.

The older prompts prescribed modern fantasy/anime rendering, painterly realism, warm woodland light and decorative sheets. Those are retained in the source archive as past presentation choices, but are not default requirements in the revised master prompt.

## Core and optional modules

Use the [Master Character Prompt](../Myca%20Master%20Character%20Prompt.md) as the core. Add the standalone paragraph from the [Locomotion Reference](../Myca%20Locomotion%20Reference.md) only for contact or movement tasks. Add the [Expression Guide](../Myca%20Expression%20and%20Acting%20Guide.md) module for acting studies. Do not paste every historical prompt into the same request.

A neutral standing model can show the clean point geometry without a terrain demonstration. A scene where she is actively anchoring should use the locomotion rules. Omitting a behaviour illustration is not a change to her anatomy.

## Standalone cap module

Myca's cap is a living extension growing from her head, based on an expanded mature fly agaric. It is enormous, wider than the shoulders and horizontally dominant, with a broad shallow dome, a low central rise and a thin, gently irregular wavy rim. Retain this shallow volume from the front, side and rear. Its crimson-to-scarlet upper surface carries a limited number of prominent irregular cream-white patches and smaller fragments, varied in size and spacing. Closely packed fine cream-to-beige radial gills fill the underside; thin edge tissue allows some light transmission. It is one attached mushroom structure, not a tall triangular crown, a narrow cone or a removable hat. Do not copy the taller cap from superseded turnarounds.

## Standalone head-turnaround module

Show the same Myca head at a consistent scale in front, front three-quarter, profile, rear three-quarter and rear views. Keep the face neutral and the cap attached. Rotate one coherent cap-and-head volume, retaining its broad shallow shape, rim undulations, patch landmarks, bob outline and attachment. Preserve the unmarked white eyes and tiny mouth seam where visible. No capless view is needed. Use a simple presentation that allows comparison; identify left and right accurately rather than duplicating a view with a different label. Any proposed patch map is a working layout until approved.

## Standalone full-body-turnaround module

Show one consistent Myca model in comparable front, front three-quarter, side, rear three-quarter and rear views, at a shared scale and neutral pose. Preserve the cap width and shallow dome, compact lichen bob, humanoid upper-leg volume, very slim calves and long uninterrupted terminal points. Align the same structural landmarks between views. Keep skirt and waistband shape consistent. Do not substitute feet in a side view or add contact webs to a neutral anatomy study. Treat the same chosen cap patch pattern and garment construction as persistent across the rotation.

## Isolated contact-study module

Show only the same lower leg and the same patch of substrate through Approach, Touch, Sprout, Explore, Anchor and Release. Keep the continuous point unchanged. Lateral hyphae appear only after contact, from immediately above the substrate, then branch irregularly along the terrain. At final release the airborne point is clean. Keep camera, scale and environmental landmarks fixed. This isolates the mechanism without asking a full character sheet to solve every feature at once.

## Living fungal-membrane skirt module

Use this module only for membrane experiments. It overrides the baseline skirt-and-waistband construction for the requested asset and does not alter Myca's body, cap, lichen, face, limbs or locomotion.

The membrane grows directly from Myca's pale mould-green body and transitions organically into dusty pink. **No belt, waistband, fastening, sewn seam, detached fabric or draped-on garment is present.** Carry subtle internal hyphae into the membrane so the material feels continuous with her body, while keeping the membrane thin enough to read as a specialised fungal sheet rather than a second torso.

Choose one architecture per variant unless a hybrid is explicitly requested:

- **Layered petal:** overlapping soft sheets emerge progressively from the hips, giving a layered petal silhouette.
- **Single-sheet:** one continuous thin flared membrane, closest to a traditional skirt silhouette but unmistakably living tissue.
- **Vascular-reinforced:** stronger branching reinforcement is concentrated at the transition and remains visible down the sheet, slowly diminishing toward the edge.
- **Gill-frill:** lamella-like radial pleats flare into repeated gill-inspired frills; ribbing is structural, not textile pleating.
- **Veil-streamer:** a partial-veil origin divides into long thin ribbons / streamers with open gaps between them.
- **Spore-lace:** a fenestrated sheet with organic holes, thin bridges and irregular porous topology; do not turn it into woven lace.
- **Shelf-fungus:** overlapping bracket-like shelves grow in tiered bands, with scalloped edges and a more semi-rigid layered mass.

For comparison sheets, make the candidates structurally distinct at silhouette scale. Do not merely change edge decoration or texture. Label all seven as **experimental candidates** unless the creator explicitly promotes one.

## Style-translation guidance

The following are production suggestions, not new facts about Myca.

| Target | Keep readable | Permissible simplification |
|---|---|---|
| Small icon or sprite | Broad red/cream cap, dark bob, pale body, pink skirt, clean pointed legs. | Reduce or omit tiny internal filaments; do not replace them with star dots. |
| Flat-colour or line drawing | Continuous leg contours, low cap profile, mouth seam and eye markings absent. | Indicate lichen with a few small lobed clusters instead of rendering every structure. |
| Monochrome work | Relative light/dark masses, cap patches, dark bob and light unmarked eyes. | Translate colour families into value relationships; keep the written colour specification alongside the asset. |
| Sculpted or simplified 3D form | One coherent cap volume, attached head, reduced calves and continuous points. | Physical translucency and eye emission may be represented rather than literally implemented. |
| Detailed surface treatment | Layered fine hyphae, material continuity and small lichen structures. | Detail must remain subordinate; do not use extra surface anatomy to create realism. |
| Animation | Stable proportions, cap landmarks, face and hair mass; clean contact-state changes. | Select a repeatable technical rig while keeping its unapproved parameters out of canon. |

Material response is not a renderer requirement. Slightly translucent tissue can be communicated by several means; glassy eyes need not imply photorealistic rendering. Warm backlight is one way to reveal thin tissue, not part of the character's base palette.

## Reference pairing examples

For a new body model, pair **R08/C01 for proportion** with **R11/C03 for terminal geometry**, then apply the thin-calf and latest cap text. For hair, **R08/C02** is a texture-and-silhouette reference, not permission to copy facial star nodes. For contact, use the **C04–C06** sequence with the written emergence-zone and clean-release rules. R17/R18 can supplement environmental interaction but do not override those rules.

Never submit an older entire sheet as “copy exactly” when it contains a feature that was later corrected. The reference registry exists to make the scope explicit.
